01Introduction

The Lumineo Project Scheduler is the shop's single, shared home for planning work — replacing the old Canvas scheduling app and the whiteboards, spreadsheets, and guesswork that came with it.

It brings three connected calendars together — Production (the shop floor), Installation (the WK & NEK field crews), and Shipping — all driven by the same scheduling engine and fed live from Business Central (BC). Everyone sees the same board, updated in real time, so the person in the office and the crew lead in the field are always looking at the same plan.

See the whole week at a glance

Every job, every person, color-coded by department — with weather, crew size, and dollar value right on the card.

Schedule by dragging

Drop a job on a day, stretch it to change its length, and let the engine keep everything downstream in order.

Plan "what-ifs" safely

Try a change in the Scenario Sandbox before it touches the live board.

Hit the monthly number

Roll up installation billing against the goal and auto-fill the month from jobs that are ready.

Who this guide is for Every team member who touches the schedule — from admins who plan it to floor and field staff who read it. Not every section applies to every role; jump to Roles & Permissions to see what your login can do.

Reading the board

  • Rows are grouped under a colored department / location banner (Metal Shop, Paint, Hutchinson, Wichita…). The banner color also tints that group's cards.
  • Columns are the seven days (Mon–Sun). Weekends are shaded because we don't usually schedule them.
  • Cards show the job number, customer, and task; a bar's width is how long the job takes. An arrow (◂ ▸) means the job continues off the visible week or skips the weekend. A job long enough to run past Friday keeps going on the next week's board — click Next › and you'll see it resume on Monday with a ◂ arrow on its left edge, for as many weeks as it runs. A card that spans several days un-stacks automatically to stay short — the job #, customer, and description move onto one line (with " | " between the customer and the description) and its task list runs together with " • " separators instead of one task per row.
  • Week navigation — use ‹ Prev / Next › to move a week at a time. On the current week, the middle button is 📅 Go to… — click it to pick any day from a calendar and jump to that week. Once you're on another week it becomes Today to jump back.
Tip Hover almost anything — a badge, an icon, a button — and a tooltip explains what it does. When in doubt, hover first.

05Features & How to Use Them

5.1  Adding a job

Click + Add Job (top-right of the toolbar) or click an empty day cell for a person. You get two kinds of card:

BC Job

Adding a BC job is now a simple two-step flow — the same job card you get when you click a scheduled job, opened up front so you set the card exactly how you want before it lands:

  1. Search & pick tasks. Search by BC job number or customer name and pick the job. Then choose Single (one task), Multi (several tasks — they stack on one card and their hours sum), or Custom task (type a one-off task for a job that has no BC planning line). Click the task(s) you want, then Continue →.
  2. Configure & schedule. The job card opens pre-filled: the task text, the estimated hours (summed from the tasks you picked), the target production / install window (from the release-date rules), and the production stage stepper (from the job's planning lines). Modified labor hours and End start blank. Tip: if you started the add by clicking a person's day cell (Production or Installation), that employee and the clicked date are already filled in for you — so it's ready to Schedule right there (via the toolbar + Add Job button both stay blank). Fill in what you know:
    • Set an employee and a start date → the button reads Schedule and drops the card exactly there.
    • Leave either blank → the button reads Auto Schedule. It places the card on the employee's first open day — filling a partial day if they're under 8h, then bleeding into later days as the hours need, or starting the first fully-free day if their scheduled days are already full. It always lands after existing work (never on top of it). With no employee chosen it picks the least-loaded person in the task's department. Installation: an auto-scheduled install always stays on its single day (installs complete the day they're scheduled, regardless of hours) — you can still manually drag-resize it across days afterward.
One way, everywhere This same Add-Job flow (search → pick tasks) is now used everywhere jobs are added — the board, the Job Queue, and group cards — so there's one consistent way to add a job. (The old Auto mode that force-selected every task has been removed; use Auto Schedule on the configure step instead.)

Scheduling several jobs at once (batch)

At the top of the BC Job panel, switch from Single job to Multiple jobs to build a prioritized list and schedule them together:

  1. Set up each job exactly as above (search → tasks → configure), but the button now reads Add to list instead of Schedule — the job is staged, not placed yet. Repeat + Add job to add as many as you want.
  2. Pre-load from the Job Queue — use Load from queue to drop every job in a queue group straight into the list at once, instead of adding them one by one.
  3. Click any job in the list to edit it — the full job card opens so you can set its employee, start date, hours, or any other field (something you couldn't do when the job only lived in the queue). Update in list saves your changes back to that row.
  4. In the list, prioritize the jobs: drag them up/down, or Sort by Release date, Production complete, Install window, or Hours (ascending/descending).
  5. Pick a Schedule from date to aim the whole list at a future week — auto-scheduled jobs start from the first opening on or after that date. Leave it blank for the next available opening.
  6. Click Schedule N jobs. They're scheduled top-to-bottom — each job lands per how you set it up (fixed employee/day, or auto onto the first open slot), and auto-scheduled jobs pack around the ones above them so your priority order is respected.

Splitting a job into sections

Sometimes a person works a job, moves onto other jobs, then comes back to it later. Rather than scheduling the job a second time — which would book its whole estimate all over again — split the card so the sections share one pot of hours.

  1. Right-click the card → Split into sections…
  2. Set the hours for each part. It opens with the card's hours divided evenly; type over them however you like (e.g. a 24h card as 16h + 8h). Use + Add part for three or more.
  3. Click Split into N. Part 1 stays exactly where it is; each other part is placed automatically at that person's next opening — packing around whatever else they already have. Drag any part wherever you want afterwards, including into a future week.

Each part shows a small 1/2, 2/2 badge on the card so you can see at a glance that it's a section of a bigger task. Open any part and it tells you where it sits: "Part 2 of 2 · this card 8h · other parts 16h · estimate 24h".

The estimate never doubles. The task's Estimated hours stays the BC number on every part — the parts divide it between them in Modified labor hours. And if you add a job task that's already partly scheduled, the new card tells you so — "8h of this task is already scheduled on Len Cook Jr (Jul 31–Aug 3)" — and pre-fills the hours box with the hours left instead of the full estimate. Type over it if it really is additional work. Going over the estimate is allowed (jobs run long); it's just flagged in red so nobody double-books a task by accident.

Working a Saturday or Sunday

Weekends are shaded and left out of scheduling by default — but you can put a card on one yourself. Click a Saturday or Sunday cell on someone's row (or drag a card onto it, or type a weekend date into Start) and the job stays there: the hours land on that day, the card ends that day, and the day counts toward that person's load.

Only the day you choose opens up. The rest of the weekend stays off, so a job that runs longer than the day picks up again on Monday rather than rolling into Sunday. And Auto Schedule never picks a weekend — even for someone already working that Saturday. Coming in on a weekend is your call to make, not the scheduler's. (If somebody works weekends as a rule, set that on their roster record instead and the scheduler will treat those days normally.)

Custom Card (blocking out time)

For PTO, holidays, inventory, truck maintenance, certifications — anything that isn't a BC job.

  1. Switch to Custom Card and pick a saved card from the dropdown (built-in ones like PTO / Holiday, plus any your team saved), or build your own.
  2. Set the title, color (use the Department / Location swatches to match the group), hours, and scope — One resource, Entire department, or All (e.g. a shop-wide Holiday).
  3. Optionally tick Lock so cascade flows around it, then Schedule.
  4. Reuse it next time by clicking ＋ Save as reusable card — it's saved for this board (Production or Installation) and shared with the team.

5.2  Moving, resizing & the cascade

  • Move — drag a card to another day or person.
  • Resize — drag the right edge of a card to set how many days it spans — longer or shorter. Whatever length you drag it to sticks (you can even make a card shorter than its hours would normally fill). This is a visual span only — it does not change the labor hours and does not pop up a dialog, so you can lay out the week quickly. The days it spans aren't reserved (other jobs can still use that person). Set the actual hours in Modified labor hours when you're ready.
  • Change start date — drag the left edge of a card to slide its start to another day (snaps to whole days within the week; a live date badge shows the new start). The job keeps its length.
  • Reorder same-day jobs (set priority) — when a person has more than one job on the same day, drag a card up or down within that day to set the order they work them, top = first. A blue insertion line shows where the card will land. The card you move takes the earlier start and the others shift later that day; if the day is already full, the job that no longer fits rolls to the next day. On Production & Installation boards.
A change only affects the card you change By default nothing auto-moves: editing, moving, or resizing a card changes only that card — no other job shifts on its own, and the board keeps its exact positions when you reload (overlaps just show a ⚡ conflict icon so you can fix them by hand). If you'd rather the board auto-arrange around your changes, turn on Auto-cascade in Settings — then a move/resize that would push other jobs shows a preview dialog (Continue / Move only this / Try in Sandbox) before nudging downstream work.

5.3  Editing a job

Click a card to open the Edit Job panel. There you can edit the job description and card text, re-pick which BC tasks the card covers (see 5.6), reassign the employee, and set the Start and End dates, the read-only Estimated hours (the task's fixed BC estimate), and Modified labor hours — the actual working hours for this card, which override the estimate (leave blank to use the estimate). Setting the End date adjusts the Modified hours to land there (in whole-day steps). Use the lock icon in the panel header to pin the card so cascade flows around it. On Installation you also set trips, men, trucks, the install ZIP, and the Final install flag (5.5). You can Duplicate the card onto other people, or Delete it.

Job details sit just below the production stepper, for everyone who opens a card. View-only users see them laid out one after another; editors get them folded under one click-to-open ▸ Job Information heading (it starts closed), since their panel also carries the edit fields. The panel scrolls when there's more than fits on screen — the Save / Cancel buttons stay at the bottom.

  • Ship-to address — the job's ship-to address from Business Central, shown as a link. Click it and choose Open in Google Maps (directions on a phone or computer) or Copy address.
  • Weather · scheduled day — for install crews: the forecast for the card's day at the job's ZIP (the same forecast as the card's hover preview).
  • ▸ Salesperson & Project Manager — click the heading to open it (it starts closed). Shows the job's BC salesperson and that salesperson's project manager.
  • ▸ Field Description and ▸ Production Description — click to open. The job's descriptions from Business Central, exactly as typed there (the same text that prints on the job traveler): the Field Description holds the install manager's / salesperson's instructions for the install crew (power lines, who to call on site…), the Production Description the production manager's instructions for the shop. Long descriptions scroll inside the section. “Nothing entered in BC” means the field is empty in BC.
  • ▸ Extended Description — editors only. The sales description used on proposals.
  • ▸ Purchase orders — click to open (it starts closed). Every purchase order written to the job in Business Central, newest first: the PO # (click it to open the order in BC), the vendor, and the date ordered. While an order is still open in BC its badge shows BC's status (Open, Released, Pending Approval…); once BC has archived it — fully received and invoiced — the badge reads Closed and the PO # opens the archived order. The list is refreshed from BC every night, so a PO created today shows up tomorrow morning. Only open jobs have their POs listed.

The descriptions are refreshed from BC every hour and the purchase orders every night, so an edit made in BC shows here within the hour (descriptions) or the next morning (POs). Reopen the card to see it.

View-only users (Production, Install, Sales, PM) see the panel down to these job details — the job and card text, tasks, target dates, stepper, address and details — and a Close button. The editing fields (employee, start / end, hours, install dates, trips and crew, Final install) only appear for editors.

The panel also shows the job's production stage (5.4), the Job tasks checklist (5.6), and — for editors — the Install Dates (5.5). View-only roles see a read-only version of the card.

5.4  Production stage & marking steps complete

Every BC job card shows a production stepper — the departments the job needs (Material Cut, Steel, Routing, Metal Fab, Paint, Vinyl / Graphics, Assembly, Crating), in flow order, plus a final Install step when the job has install work. The departments come from the job's BC planning lines; Crating is added whenever a planning line is crating labor. Material Cut has no planning line — editors add it with the stepper's Edit. In BC, Material Cut is the Substrate Cut/Prep step and Crating is Crating. Each node shows its standing: completed, active (in progress, red), or still to come (navy).

  • Mark a step done — anyone can complete a department. Click the department on the stepper — it glows yellow-orange — then click the blue Complete button that appears underneath. Each completion is stamped with who and when, listed under Completed below the stepper. Click a completed department to see its stamp; editors get a Reopen button there.
  • When a step completes, that card recedes on the board, and the department's tasks in the Job tasks list (5.6) grey out as done.
  • Finishing the last department marks the job complete in Business Central. When every department on a job's stepper is done, the scheduler writes that back to BC — the job is flagged complete and stamped with the completion date. You don't do anything extra; it happens on the click that closes the last step. Reopening a department un-flags it in BC the same way. Note the job's BC status is left alone — only the complete flag and date are written, so nothing gets posted or billed on your behalf.
  • The board keeps BC's Project Planning up to date. Each department's work on a job is mirrored to the matching step in the job's BC Project Planning list — no extra clicks:
    • Dates — when you add, move, resize or delete a card, BC's step gets the department's whole window: from the start of its earliest card to the end of its latest one, across every week, in Central time.
    • Who — the step's Assigned To is set when every card on it is the same person. Several people, or a team lane, leaves BC's assignee as it was.
    • Which department it's in — BC's Started box follows the stepper's active department(s). That's what puts the project in that department's tile in BC. Complete a department and the next one becomes active, so BC moves the project to the next department's tile on its own. Set active on several departments and it shows in each of their tiles. A department that stops being active without being finished is un-Started again.
    • Done — completing a department here marks its BC step Complete; Reopen clears it again. Where several departments share one BC step (Steel, Metal Fab and Fabrication Help all feed Fabrication), the step is Complete only once all of them are. Scheduling a card never changes Started or Complete.
    • The main steps — BC's Production step is marked Started as soon as any production department is, and Complete once every production department on the stepper is done. Installation/Service is Started once Install becomes the active step (production done, or the job moved to an Installation status) and Complete when Install is completed (which the Complete statuses do).
    Departments map to BC steps as: Steel, Metal Fab and Fabrication Help → Fabrication; Routing → Routing; Paint → Painting; Vinyl / Graphics → Vinyl; Assembly → Final Assembly; Material Cut → Substrate Cut/Prep; Crating → Crating; Install → Install. If the job has no row for that step in BC yet, one is added. Deleting a department's last card leaves BC's dates as they were. Changes reach BC within a minute or two.
  • At a glance — hover any job card (Production or Installation, and the job pills inside a group card) to see the stepper right in the preview, so you can read a job's production progress without opening it.
  • Editors (Admin / Ops / Developer) can tailor the stepper: click Edit to add a missing department (or Install), and with a department selected use the red Delete button to remove a node or Set active to flag extra departments as active (so more than one can be in progress at once). These edits are saved per job.

Completing a step from the shop floor (BC job punches)

Employees can complete their department right from the Business Central punch screens — no one has to click it in the app:

  • Moving to the next job (the usual way): Clock In Project always shows Currently on and a Complete current task checkbox. When you're already punched into a job it reads Currently on: J12345 / task and the checkbox works — tick it before you punch into the new job if you finished the one you're leaving. When you aren't punched in (first punch of the day, back from lunch) it reads Not clocked in to a project and the checkbox is greyed out.
  • Clocking out (lunch, end of day): Clock Out Project has a Task complete checkbox — tick it if you finished the task you're clocking out of.
  • On several projects at once: Clock In Multiple Projects/Nestings and Clock Out Multiple Projects/Nestings show a Mark tasks complete list under the open punches — one row per project you're punched into, each with its own Task complete checkbox. Tick every project you finished, then Submit (clocking out, or clocking into a new project). Each ticked project is completed separately. A tick also counts if the punch is closed some other way later in the day. History shows these as Multi Clock Out / Multi Clock In.

Within a few minutes the job moves on to the next stage of its job flow (below). When that was the department's last stage, the department completes on the job's stepper (stamped Punch · employee name), the next department becomes active and BC Project Planning follows. Either way the job's Current Status moves to the status of its next stage — e.g. completing Metal Fab moves a job from MFG - Len Metal Fab to MFG - Paint Prep / Paint. Jobs on a hold or a special status (Service, Morton, Billboards, Refurb, Complete to Admin…) keep their status — only the stepper moves. Editors can always change a job's status by hand afterwards. Each job's ticks, and what the app did with them, are listed in the Jobs list's job panel under Shop floor · Task complete.

Job flow: the order and statuses a job moves through

A job's flow is its list of stages, top to bottom — each a stepper department plus the Current Status it means. A department can have more than one stage when it has several statuses, e.g. Vinyl / Graphics → MFG - Vinyl Cut, then MFG - Vinyl Application:

  • A shop-floor Task complete completes the job's next stage of that department. On the first Vinyl tick the status moves to MFG - Vinyl Application and Vinyl stays open on the stepper; the second tick completes Vinyl and moves the job on to Assembly.
  • If someone already set the status to a later stage by hand, the next tick counts from there.
  • A tick for a department the job hasn't reached yet (working ahead) is recorded but doesn't change the status.
  • The flow's order is also the stepper's order: put Paint before Metal Fab and Paint goes active first, and BC is told the same.

Settings → Job flow (Admin / Ops / Developer) sets the company flow every job starts from: reorder stages with ↑ / ↓, change a stage's status, remove one with ✕, or Add a stage (pick the department and status — it goes right after that department's other stages), plus the status once every stage is done. Each job uses only the departments on its own stepper, in this order.

To change one job, open it from the Jobs list and click ▸ Job flow under the stepper. It shows where the job is now (●) and which stages are done (✓); editors can reorder and change the stages there for that job only, and Back to the company flow undoes it. Everyone else can see the job's flow but not change it.

Keeping on top of the automatic moves:

  • A status a punch moved gets an AUTO tag beside it in the Jobs list and the job panel (hover it for who and when). Once you've checked it, click its × to clear the tag — the status stays. Picking a status by hand also clears it. (Clearing the tag needs the right to change Current Status.)
  • History (the clock button left of Refresh in the Jobs toolbar) lists every tick from the last 30 days, newest first: the date and time, job (click to open it), resource, task, the department it completed, the step the job moved to, and the Current Status change (old → new, or Unchanged). Ticks the app couldn't use show as Skipped with the reason. The number on the button is how many jobs still have an AUTO tag. Filter with All / Status moves / Needs review / Skipped; ✓ Reviewed on a row (or Mark all reviewed) clears the AUTO tags.
Good to know The department comes from the job task's planning lines (or the employee's own department when the task covers several). Ticks are applied by the app of an Admin / Ops / Developer who has it open — during the day that's within minutes; a tick made overnight is applied when the office opens the app in the morning.

5.5  Target & install dates

Every BC job card shows a Target dates section (in the Edit Job panel, between the card text and the production stepper — and on the card's hover preview). Both show the same dates as the Jobs list (§5.17): Mfg target (the Jobs list's Mfg Final — marked (modified) when a modified date replaces the target) and the Scheduled install, or the Install target until install is scheduled (a Red date also shows). Jobs not on the Jobs list use the computed guides below. These are guides, not committed dates:

  • Target production complete — the job's production lead time after its release date: 7 weeks by default, or what a lead-time rule in Settings → Lead times sets for jobs with its combination of steps (out of the box, vinyl/graphics-only jobs get 4 weeks).
  • Est. install window — starts the working day after production completes and runs until the job's install lead time (10 weeks after release by default, so a 3-week window). As soon as you set a Scheduled install, the window is replaced by that committed day (which then shows on the job card).

When a Red date is set, it appears as a red bar above the Target dates section (Red Date: mm/dd/yy). The anchor for the targets is the Release date — the job's order-release date from Business Central (shown in Install Dates; type a date to override per job, or clear it to fall back to the BC date). Three things shift the production target:

  • A Red date — always wins: production target = the working day before the Red date (production has to finish before the drop-dead install).
  • A Scheduled install — with no Red date, production target = the working day before the scheduled install day.
  • Production completion (override) — a field (between the End date and Install Dates) to set the production-complete date by hand when there's no committed install. Clear it to use the computed date.

All targets land on working days — a target that would fall on a weekend moves forward to Monday (never back to Friday).

Install dates & the Red date

Editors open Install Dates in the Edit Job panel with the eye icon (it shows/hides the fields; the whole section is hidden for view-only roles):

  • Release date — the job's order-release date from Business Central; anchors the target dates (5.5). Type to override per job; clear to fall back to the BC date.
  • Scheduled install — the job's committed install day.
  • Red date — a fixed, drop-dead install date. Setting it locks the scheduled date to match and gives the job a pulsing red outline on every board it appears on.

On an Installation card, tick Final install to make that card's day set the job's scheduled install date automatically — so the schedule and the install date stay in sync as you move the final card.

Shipment (truck) cards

A 🚚 shipment card on the Installation board is a shipping load — one truck run that can carry several stops. Click it to open the load's item list. The header shows the load name, its ship date, status, and how many items it holds; a Stops line lists the load's locations.

Each stop is its own card showing:

  • the job # (or "No job #" for a stop that isn't tied to a BC job),
  • a → Delivery or ↩ Pickup badge,
  • the customer / job name and a description,
  • the labeled delivery / pickup location, and any notes.

Use Edit card… in that popup to change the card itself (editors). The stops are listed in the load's own order — set that on the Shipping board (see 5.15).

5.6  Job tasks checklist

The Edit Job panel includes a collapsible Job tasks list — the job's BC planning-line tasks, with a summary of how many are on the card and how many are done. In the list:

  • Highlighted rows are the tasks currently on this card. Ticking / unticking a task re-links the card's text and hours (editors).
  • Greyed, struck-through rows with a ✓ done tag are complete — a task greys automatically once its department is marked done on the production stepper (5.4).

5.7  Undo / Redo

Made a mistake? Use the ↶ / ↷ buttons in the week summary, or Ctrl+Z / Ctrl+Y. Up to 20 steps per board. History clears when the board reloads (e.g., changing weeks).

5.8  The Job Queue

A slide-out holding area (toggle it from the week summary — editors only) for jobs that need scheduling but don't have a slot yet. Organize jobs into colored groups ("Needs Scheduled", "Ready to Schedule"…), then drag a card straight onto the board — or drag a scheduled card back into the queue to un-schedule it.

Adding a job to the queue — click + Add Job in a group. This opens the same Add Job panel the board uses (search → Single / Multi / Custom task). Pick your task(s) and click Add to queue — the job is parked in the queue (with its task text + summed hours) ready to drag onto the board whenever you're ready.

From BC steps — groups that fill themselves

At the top of the queue, From BC steps lists every tracked job by the step it's in right now — the same jobs Business Central shows in its Project Planning step tiles. On the Production board there's a group per step (Substrate Cut/Prep, Fabrication, Routing, Painting, Vinyl, Final Assembly, Crating — a Crating card drops onto the Assembly lane); on the Installation boards, Ready for Install holds that region's jobs whose production is done.

  • Header colours — editors hover a step's header and click ✎ to pick its colour (a swatch, or Custom color for any background and text colour); Use default colour puts it back. The colours are shared by everyone and show on both the Production and Installation boards.
  • A job is listed under a step when that department is active on its stepper. Complete the department and the job moves to the next group straight away.
  • Each card carries the job's BC tasks and hours for that step. Drag it onto the board to schedule it — it makes a normal card, exactly like the Add Job panel would.
  • A job that's already on the calendar for that step stays listed but faded, tagged Scheduled. The count on each group reads to schedule / active.
  • These groups are read-only — they come from the steppers, so you can't rename them or park jobs in them. Your own groups below work as before.

Grouped job cards

A group card is a container you drop on the board to hold a list of BC jobs — handy for "waiting on parts", a punch-list, or any set of jobs a crew can pick up together. It has a title, an optional description (hidden from non-editors when left blank), and shows each member job as a pill inside the card. It auto-colors to the department / location it sits on, and you can move it (dragging sets its start day) and resize it like any card.

  • Add one — click + Add Job (or an empty day cell), switch to the Group Card tab, and give it a title. To add member jobs, click + Add a job — this opens the same Add Job panel used everywhere (search → Single / Multi / Custom task); pick task(s) and click Add to group. To place the card on a whole department instead of one person, right-click the department banner → Add group card…
  • View a job in the group — hover a pill for a standard job-card preview, or click it to open a detail popover with Open Project and Open SharePoint Folder links — just like a regular job card.
  • Edit the group — click the card's background to open its list, where editors can rename it, edit the description, add / remove jobs, or delete the whole group.
  • Size limit — a group holds roughly 15 jobs. If it's full, the editor stops you from adding more (with a note) — remove a job or start a second group card.

Group cards replaced the old per-department "Fill-in Jobs" list — same idea, now a real card you can place, color, and move anywhere on the board.

5.9  Roster management (crews & departments)

  • Edit / delete — right-click a person's name.
  • Hours per day & time efficiency (Production) — right-click a person's name to set their Hours / day and Time efficiency %. Efficiency scales their usable hours: e.g. 8h/day at 80% = 6.4 available hours per day (shown live as you type). 5.10  Assist Installation (lending people across departments)

Some days a Production person works with an install crew. Right-click their name → Assist Installation, pick the region (WK / NEK) and the days — each day can be All, AM, or PM. On the Installation board they show as a crew row for that week, with a Production filler on the days/halves they're back in the shop so their row doesn't look empty. Come back anytime to Edit days (drop a day or change AM/PM) or Remove assist.

A loan is for one week only. The person appears on the Installation roster for the week you lent them and no other — page forward and they're gone. (They used to linger on every future week's Installation board; that's fixed.) To lend them again next week, set the assist up on that week.

Their install jobs now show on the Production board too

A lent person can plan their whole week from their own board. Their install jobs are drawn on their Production row, on the days they're lent, each badged with how much of the day it takes:

  • Install — a whole day with the install crew.
  • Install AM / Install PM — only that half; the other half is still theirs for Production work.

These cards have a dashed edge to set them apart from Production jobs, and are read-only here — hover or click for the detail, but move, resize and delete are off, because the job belongs to the Installation board. Change it there and Production follows.

A full lent day still blocks Production scheduling exactly as before — you just now see what they're doing instead of a plain grey "Installation" block. An AM/PM day still leaves the other half open for a Production job.

Putting a shipping load on a Production employee

Most of the company reads the Installation board to see what's being installed and what's going out on a truck. So a load run by someone in Production needs to be on both boards — and now it is:

  • On Production, + Add Job → Custom Card now has the same Shipment load picker the Installation board has. Pick the load, pick the person, and Schedule.
  • The load is created on the Installation board as a 🚚 truck card, and that person is added to the Installation schedule for that day — just as if you'd lent them.
  • It comes straight back onto their Production row too, so their week reads correctly from either board.

A shipment card doesn't get an Install badge — the blue 🚚 truck card already says it's a load. And this only works one way: an Installation employee scheduled for a load stays on the Installation board, and does not appear on Production.

5.11  Scenario Sandbox

A safe copy of the board for "what-if" planning. Make changes, see the impact, and only commit them to the live schedule when you're happy — or discard them. Admin/Ops only.

5.12  Monthly Plan (gameplanning) & auto-fill

Install billing across both regions for the current billing month, with a card per week and that month's goal. The header shows which installs count — e.g. "October 2026 billing · installs ending Sep 25 – Oct 23" — plus what's Booked, WK / NEK, progress and the Gap to the goal. Admin/Ops only.

  • Booked is every job on the WK and NEK install boards, counted once at its calendar value (BC remaining balance, else the card's invoice amount), in the week its install ends (its last card). Each week card shows its jobs on hover and the crew hours booked against what the crews have (40 h a crew a week). ↻ Refresh re-reads the boards.
  • Three pools of jobs from the Jobs list:
    • Past due — not installed yet, and the date it was needed by has passed (its RED date, else Sched. Install, else Install Target), most late first. It shows whether the job is on an install board or not scheduled at all. These need attention now.
    • Ready for install — production is done (Install is the active stepper step) and the job isn't on an install board yet.
    • Near complete — one production step left and not on an install board yet.
    Each shows the install hours and crew size from the job's BC install planning lines (e.g. WK 2 MAN) and its value. Install hours are crew-hours for a 2-man crew: every trip's crew line and travel are added up, and a bigger crew line (e.g. 4 MAN) counts once per 2 men. Past-due jobs that are still early in production stay in the Past due list but aren't auto-filled.
  • ✨ Auto-fill plans Ready and Near-complete jobs into this month's weeks, from this week on: past-due jobs first, then by needed-by date, then the bigger value. A job goes in the first week with room for its $ (toward the weekly target) and its install hours (that region's crew hours less what's booked); a near-complete job goes no earlier than the week after its Mfg Final date. Jobs it couldn't place are listed with the reason.
  • In the proposal, untick anything you don't want and press Commit. Each job's Sched. Install is set to the Monday of its week (shown on the Jobs list and the job's Install Dates), and it's added to a "Planned · week of …" group in that region's Install Job Queue, ready to drag onto the board for the exact crew and day. Committing needs schedule edit access.

Billing months follow the billing cut-off, not the calendar. A job counts toward the month its install ends in, and it has to end the day before that month's cut-off so the order can be turned in — an install that ends on the cut-off day counts toward the next month. The cut-off dates and each month's goal are set in Settings → Billing periods (5.14). The Installation board's Billing stat (e.g. Billing · Oct) uses the same months; hover it to see the dates.

5.13  My Schedule / My Active Jobs

Sales & PMs get My Active Jobs — every job they own and where it currently sits across Production, Installation, and Shipping; click a job to open its read-only job card (production stage, tasks, and schedule).

5.15  The Shipping board — building a load

The Shipping board (sidebar) plans truck runs by day. A load is one truck run, holding as many stops as it needs. Each load also appears as a 🚚 shipment card on the Installation board (5.5), where the crew can open it read-only.

  • Create a load — + Add Load in the toolbar, or + Add load under any day.
  • Click a load to open its editor: route name, ship day (use ‹ / › to reach another week), status (Planned → Ready → Loaded → Delivered), and load notes.
  • Name — left alone, the name writes itself from the date and stops ("8/11 Dodge City & Garden City Load"). Type your own to override it; clear it to go back to automatic.
  • Add items — search a BC job to pull in its job #, customer, and description, or + Manual item for something with no job (transformers, a pickup, materials). Set each item's Deliver / Pickup, location, and loading notes, and tick items off as they're loaded.
  • Print gives the driver a shipping list of the load, in order.

The printed sheet has a wide Notes column with room to write on the truck. Anything typed into an item's loading notes is printed there; the rest of the column is blank space for the driver.

Choosing the tick boxes that print

Every line on the printed sheet gets a tick box per column — out of the box, Loaded and Order. You choose which columns print, and you can build up your own list of them:

  • On the print preview, open the Tick boxes ▾ menu (top right).
  • Tick any column in the list to put it on the sheet, untick to take it off.
  • Add your own — type a name in the box at the bottom (Strapped, Tarped, Paperwork, Photo…) and press Enter or Add. It's saved to the list for next time and ticked straight away.
  • × next to a name you added removes it from the list. Loaded and Order are always offered, so they can only be unticked, not deleted.

Your choice sticks and applies to every load you print from this device — set it once and forget it. Bear in mind each tick column takes width from the Notes column: two or three leaves plenty of writing room, and the sheet stops you at six.

Putting the items in the order you want

Items are numbered 1, 2, 3… down the left — the order they're loaded, and the order they print on the driver's sheet. Set that order yourself:

  • Drag the ⠿ grip at the left of an item and drop it where you want it. The row you're about to displace highlights as you drag.
  • Or use the keyboard — click a grip to focus it, then press ↑ / ↓ to walk the item up or down the list.

Sequence the load the way it gets unloaded — last stop first, first stop last — and the printed sheet matches how the truck is packed. Reordering an auto-named load can re-word its name, since the name lists the stops in order.

Adding or changing an item's job number

The job # button on an item (or + Job # when it doesn't have one yet) attaches or changes the BC job — useful when a manual item turns out to have a job, or when the wrong job was picked.

  • Click it, search by job # or customer, and pick a result — the item's customer and description update to that job's details. Your location, loading notes, and loaded tick stay as they are.
  • Need a job the search can't find yet? Type the number and press Enter to record it as typed; the customer and description you already have are left alone.
  • Clear detaches the job number and leaves the item as a manual one.

5.16  Staging — projects waiting for a truck

Under the week sits the Staging board: a set of lists holding projects that are built and waiting to ship, before anyone has decided which run they go on. It's the shipping equivalent of the Job Queue on the calendars (5.8), except it's always on screen rather than a panel you open.

It's one master board, the same on every week. Paging the week above with ‹ Prev / Next › doesn't change it — a project sits in staging until you put it on a load, no matter which week you're looking at.

Your own lists

The lists are yours to define — name them however you plan: by readiness (Ready to Ship, Waiting on Parts), by destination (Dodge City Run, NEK), by anything else that helps.

  • Click the pencil at the right of the Staging bar to turn on list editing.
  • + Add list creates one — give it a name and a header color from the swatches, or mix your own background and text color.
  • On each header, ✎ renames or recolors it, ✕ deletes it (you're asked first, and told how many staged projects go with it), and the ⠿ grip drags the whole list left or right to reorder the board.
  • The ▾ caret on a header collapses a list you're not using, so it stops taking up width.
  • Click the pencil again when you're done — the edit controls hide and the board goes back to normal.

Putting projects into staging

  • + Add project at the bottom of a list, then search a BC job # or customer and pick it — the job number, customer, and description come along.
  • Need something BC can't find yet? Type it and press Enter to stage it by name.
  • Click a card to edit it — attach or change its job #, fix the customer, reword the description. ✕ on a card removes it from staging.

Ordering and moving cards

  • Drag a card up or down inside its list to set the order you want to work through it — it drops above whichever card you release it on.
  • Drag a card into another list to move it — say from Waiting on Parts across to Ready to Ship once it's finished.

Putting a staged project on a truck

This is the point of the board. Drag a card up onto the week:

  • Drop it on a day (any empty space in that day's column) — a new load is created on that day with the project already on it, and the load editor opens so you can set the stop location, Deliver/Pickup, and loading notes.
  • Drop it on an existing load — the project is added to that load and the editor opens on it. The load card outlines in red as you hover it, so you can see which one will take the drop.
  • Either way the project leaves the staging board — it's on a truck now, so it's no longer waiting for one.

Staging deliberately doesn't carry a stop location or Deliver/Pickup: those are decisions about this particular run, which is why the load editor opens the moment you drop. Fill them in there and the printed sheet is ready for the driver.

Taking a project back off a load

Changed your mind, or the load got too full? Send a project back to staging without losing it:

  • Open the load, then drag the item by its ⠿ grip out of the panel and drop it on whichever staging list you want it in. The page behind the panel un-dims while you drag, so you can see where it's going.
  • The item leaves the load and comes back as a staged card, ready to go on a different run.

Dropping the item inside the panel still just reorders it within the load — same grip, and where you let go decides which one you meant. Note that its stop location, Deliver/Pickup and loading notes are not kept: they described the run it just left, so you set them fresh next time it goes out.

5.17  Jobs — tracking every job (preview)

The Jobs screen (sidebar) is the job list that is replacing the Airtable LNI Production Schedule / Expeditor. Every open BC job is on it automatically, from the moment the job is opened in Business Central, with its tracking details alongside: Current Status, order date, Mfg Target / Mfg Final, Install Target, RED DATE, DIP / DOH / Actual DIP, hold, expeditor date, vendor / P.O. / vendor status / storage, date installed, date to Admin, UL sign, and more.

Who can use it: everyone can open the Jobs list. Admin / Ops / Developer can change it — the tracking columns, status, sketches, custom fields and the shared views. Everyone else gets it view only (the toolbar says so): pick a view, search, and use Filter, Sort and Group to look at the list your way — those changes are just for you and reset when you reload, so nobody else's view moves — and click a job to read its details. Adding, renaming, reordering or deleting views, the Fields button, editing a cell and changing a sketch are editor-only.

Letting someone edit certain fields: an admin can give one person edit rights to specific fields only — Settings → Users → Manage users…, click that person's Jobs button (it reads Jobs: view, or Jobs: 3 fields once some are given), tick the fields (e.g. Vendor Status, Date Installed, a custom field; also Current Status, Job name, Stepper or Sketch) and Save. That person can then change exactly those cells — their toolbar reads You can edit 3 fields — and nothing else: every other field, the views, Fields and field options stay view only for them. The job panel's Install Dates box stays editor-only; a person given one of those dates changes it in its column on the list. Admin / Operations / Developer show Jobs: all — they edit everything by role. $ columns (Value, Remaining Balance, and any Currency custom field) only show for people who can see $.

Filled in from Business Central — you don't type these:

  • Name — the job's ship-to customer (or the customer folder on the job's SharePoint link when BC has no name). Editors can Rename a job in its panel; Use BC name puts the default back.
  • Sales — the salesperson's initials (NH, VB…; LNI for the house accounts). Region follows the salesperson: Quintin Totta, Shelly Poppelreiter, Virginia Baumgartner and Jim Lyle are NEK, everyone else WK. Location is the ship-to city and state.

  • Value is the job's total — its Sales Order amount (before tax); Remaining Balance is what's left to bill. (Value falls back to the invoice amount on the job's cards until BC has the order amount.)
  • Mfg Target = release + the job's production lead time, Install Target = release + its install lead time (7 / 10 weeks, or a lead-time rule — the Lead Time Rule column shows which, Settings → Lead times). Mfg Target Mod is the production-completion override from the job's Install Dates (or the job's Airtable Mfg Final date). Mfg Final works like the Airtable formula: the modified date when there is one that differs, otherwise Mfg Target.
  • Views on the left — All Jobs, Not tracked yet (new BC jobs nobody has set up), team views (OPS / MFG, OPS / Install, Warehouse Coord.) and WK Expeditor, which matches the Airtable columns. Change them: + Add view under any section, double-click a view or section name to rename it, hover a view for duplicate (⧉) or delete (✕), drag views (even into another section) and sections to reorder them, and + Add section at the bottom. Views are shared: each view keeps its own columns, filters, sorts, groups and collapsed groups for everyone, so a change you make to a view is what the whole team sees in it (make your own view if you want one to yourself). Which view you last had open, and column widths, stay on your device.
  • Fields — choose which columns a view shows: tick / untick, search, Show all / Hide all, and drag the ⠿ grip to put the shown columns in order.
  • Filter — stack conditions with and / or; for badge columns pick the values from a list (is any of). Sort — click a column header, or use the Sort button for several levels. Group — nest groups (e.g. Current Status, then Region); click a group bar to collapse it — it stays collapsed in that view (for everyone) until someone expands it.
  • Right-click a job's name for Open Project (the job in Business Central) and Open SharePoint Folder — the same links as a calendar card's right-click menu.
  • Sketch column — a thumbnail of the job's sketch from its SharePoint folder, so you can tie the design to the job at a glance. Hover it for a bigger preview; click it to open the file full size over the dimmed list (PDFs page by page), with Download, Open in SharePoint and, for editors, Remove at the bottom — press Esc or click the dark area to close it. Remove (also Remove File at the bottom of the cell's right-click menu) asks you to confirm, then takes the sketch off the Jobs list only: nothing is deleted from SharePoint, and the nightly search won't pick a file for that job again until you choose or upload one (or pick Use the automatic pick in Choose a different file…). The sketch is the newest PDF in the job's folder (or its subfolders) whose name starts with the job number (e.g. J38740 YMCA_Wall Sign.pdf); with no such PDF, the newest picture file. Blank when the folder has neither. Sketches are picked up overnight. Wrong file, or none? A chosen file stays the sketch until you change it.
  • Stepper column — the job's production stepper, the same one as on the job card. It replaces Airtable's / (department needed) and X (department done) columns. Editors click a job's stepper to update it right in the list: a pop-up opens with the same controls as the job panel — click a department, then Complete / Reopen / Set active, or Edit to add or remove departments. Changes reach BC the same way. Esc or a click outside closes it.
  • Drag a column edge to resize it (double-click to reset); a column keeps that width in every view and on every row. Job # / Name sizes itself to the longest name in the list until you resize it by hand; double-click its edge to go back to that. A not in BC tag means the job was carried over from Airtable but isn't in the BC job sync.
  • Click a job to open it: its Tracking fields (Priority, Sales, Hold, Expeditor, Date to Admin, Vendor… — editable right there for anyone allowed to edit them, with the same pick-lists as the list), where it sits on the Production, Installation and Shipping boards, and the same production stepper and Install Dates as the job card on the boards. It's one system — completing a department or changing a date here updates the boards (and Business Central's Project Planning, §5.4) exactly as it would from a card, and changes made on the boards show here straight away.

Current Status and what it does

Editors change a job's Current Status from the drop-down at the top of its panel. Some statuses do more than change the label:

  • Complete-need paperwork, Complete to Admin, Complete Invoiced, Service Complete to Admin — complete every step on the stepper.
  • Installation, Install - waiting on product — complete every production step, so Install becomes the active step.
  • Moving into a hold status (Hold - Customer, Service - Hold…) fills Date to Hold with today and records the hold; moving out of it fills Date off Hold. A later hold starts a new one, and DOH keeps counting the earlier holds' days.
  • The steps it completes reach BC exactly like completing them on the stepper.

11FAQs & Tips

Do my changes save automatically?

Yes. Every move, resize, add, edit, and delete saves to the shared database in the background — there's no "Save" button for the board.

What if a change doesn't save?

Your change stays on screen and the app retries in the background. If it still can't save, a red "1 change hasn't saved" box appears in the bottom-right corner naming what didn't go through, with a Try again button. Nothing you did is thrown away or quietly undone.

This is the fix for the old annoyance where an action "didn't take" the first time and you had to repeat it — the board would reload and wipe out what you'd just done. It no longer reloads behind you: if you don't see that red box, it saved.

Will other people see my changes right away?

Changes save immediately; teammates see them the next time their board loads or refreshes.

What's the difference between locking a card and turning cascade off?

Locking protects one card. Cascade off is a board-wide mode that stops all auto-moving.

Can I undo after I change weeks?

Undo history is per-board and clears on reload (including week changes), so undo what you need before navigating away.

How do the crew size and weather get on the card?

Crew is derived from the job's BC planning lines (or entered by hand, which wins); weather comes from the install ZIP's forecast.

I'm on my phone — is anything different?

Everything works; the layout adapts. The sidebar becomes a hamburger menu and card details stack vertically.
